Who can join First Tech Federal Credit Union

Employees and family of hundreds of technology companies (Microsoft, Amazon, HP, Intel and many more), residents of Lane County, Oregon — or anyone in the US via membership in the Computer History Museum or the Financial Fitness Association.

Easiest path in: Anyone can join: add Computer History Museum membership (about $15, one-time, handled in the application) if you lack a tech-employer connection. (cost: $5 association/museum route makes it effectively open (small one-time fee))

Our take

What most people miss is that you don't need a tech employer to join: a one-time Computer History Museum or Financial Fitness Association membership, handled during the application, opens the door to anyone in the country. That puts First Tech on the short national list of effectively open credit unions, alongside PenFed, Alliant, and Connexus.

Day to day it behaves like a competent digital-first institution with a Northwest branch spine. The Dividend Rewards checking pays a strong rate on the first $1,000, a nice perk rather than a wealth strategy. The reason to choose First Tech is lending sophistication, not deposit yield.

Pros

  • Open to anyone via a small one-time museum/association membership
  • Mortgage underwriting that actually understands RSUs and tech compensation
  • High-rate rewards checking on the first $1,000
  • Branches inside major tech campuses in the Northwest

Cons

  • Best checking rate caps at a low balance
  • Branch network is thin outside the Pacific Northwest tech corridor
  • Some products push you toward e-statements and direct deposit to avoid fees

Is your money safe at First Tech Federal Credit Union?

Yes. First Tech Federal Credit Union is federally insured by the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA). Your deposits are protected up to $250,000 per share owner, per insured credit union, for each account ownership category — the same dollar protection FDIC insurance gives bank customers.
