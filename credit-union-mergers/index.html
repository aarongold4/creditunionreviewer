Credit Union Mergers and Name Changes: What They Mean for Your Account (2026)

By Nathan Blake · Last verified 2026-11-23

Credit unions merge and rename more often than most members realize. If a letter just told you your credit union is becoming part of another one, or your app suddenly shows a new logo, here is what typically changes, what doesn't, and what to check.

Recent mergers and renames among the credit unions we cover

We re-check every credit union on this site against its own website and the NCUA's records. As of November 2026, these are the mergers, renames and charter changes among the credit unions we cover, each confirmed on the credit union's own announcements:

  • First Tech and DCU merged effective January 1, 2026. The combined credit union is First Technology Federal Credit Union, headquartered in San Jose, California. First Tech and DCU still operate as two divisions, with their own websites and routing numbers, until they fully combine operations. See our First Tech and DCU reviews.
  • Ent and Wings merged effective January 1, 2026. The combined credit union is Wings Credit Union, doing business as Ent Credit Union, and keeps headquarters in both Colorado Springs, Colorado, and Apple Valley, Minnesota. Ent's own website says Ent "has officially changed its name to Wings Credit Union" and keeps doing business as Ent Credit Union in Colorado while it moves to the new name. See Ent and Wings.
  • UFCU and MSUFCU agreed to combine (announced, not yet complete). University Federal Credit Union in Austin, Texas, announced on October 13, 2026 that it will combine with MSU Federal Credit Union, with the merger expected in summer 2027 pending regulatory and member approval. The combined credit union would use MSUFCU's charter and the corporate name University Federal Credit Union, operating as UFCU in Texas and MSUFCU in Michigan and Illinois. UFCU says members see no immediate changes. See UFCU and MSUFCU.
  • Greater Nevada merged into America First effective October 1, 2026, according to Greater Nevada's own announcement. It keeps the Greater Nevada Credit Union name as a division of America First Credit Union, headquartered in Riverdale, Utah. See Greater Nevada and America First.
  • Premier Members merged into Meritrust, with August 1, 2025 as the legal first day of the combined credit union, which now serves members in Kansas and Colorado. See Meritrust.
  • EastRise began as a merger: New England Federal Credit Union and Vermont State Employees Credit Union legally merged on January 1, 2023, then adopted the EastRise Credit Union name in 2024. See EastRise.
  • Renames, same credit union: Bethpage Federal Credit Union became FourLeaf Federal Credit Union (March 2025); APCO Employees Credit Union rebranded as Southern Energy Credit Union (February 2025); Wescom Credit Union rebranded as Wescom Financial (April 2025); Teachers Credit Union in Indiana is now Everwise Credit Union; and Pelican State Credit Union in Louisiana dropped "State" to become Pelican Credit Union.
  • Charter conversion: Citadel announced on March 2, 2026 that it had converted from a community charter to a federal multiple common bond charter, to broaden who can join.

Merger vs. rename: why the difference matters

A rename is cosmetic. The legal entity, charter, insurance and your accounts stay the same; you mostly see a new logo, website address and card design.

A merger combines two credit unions into one legal institution with one NCUA charter. Even when both brands keep running for a while, they are a single insured credit union underneath.

What a merger can mean for your deposit insurance

NCUA share insurance covers up to $250,000 per share owner, per insured credit union, per ownership category. After a merger, the two former credit unions count as one insured institution, so balances you hold at both are added together. Coverage is usually not combined overnight: First Tech's merger FAQ, for example, described a six-month grace period after its January 1, 2026 merger before combined coverage applied. If your combined balance in any ownership category is near the limit, check your coverage with the credit union or at ncua.gov, and ask how any transition period applies to your accounts.

A checklist when your credit union merges or renames

  • Account and routing numbers. Ask whether either will change and when. That determines whether you need to update direct deposits and automatic payments.
  • Cards and apps. New debit cards, a new app or new online-banking credentials are common during conversions. Watch for official notices, and never give login details to anyone who contacts you about "updating" your account.
  • Rates and fees. The combined credit union may move to one rate sheet and fee schedule. Existing certificates usually keep their rate until maturity, but confirm it.
  • Membership. Current members normally stay members. Eligibility for new members can widen after a merger or a charter change, as Citadel's conversion shows.
  • Bookmarks. Use the credit union's new official site, and type it yourself rather than following links in unexpected emails or texts.

The bottom line

A rename changes little beyond the branding. A merger can change your account details, your rates and how much of your money is insured, so read the member notices and confirm the specifics with the credit union.
