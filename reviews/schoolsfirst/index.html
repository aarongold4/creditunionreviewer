SchoolsFirst Federal Credit Union Review (2026)

By Nathan Blake · Last verified 2026-06-07

SchoolsFirst is the country's largest education-sector credit union and one of the clearest examples of a niche charter done well. Its product design assumes you get paid ten months a year: summer saver accounts smooth income across the gap, and its planning tools are built for school-district pay calendars rather than retrofitted from generic bank products.

Who can join SchoolsFirst Federal Credit Union

Current and retired employees of California public and private schools, colleges, and education-related organizations, plus immediate family members of existing members.

Easiest path in: None (cost: $5 minimum deposit)

Our take

Membership is genuinely restricted — you need a connection to California education, either directly or through family. There is no donation path. But the definition is broader than 'teacher': classified staff, college employees, school vendors' staff in some cases, and retirees all count, and one family member unlocks eligibility for the rest.

Member satisfaction surveys routinely place SchoolsFirst near the top of all US financial institutions, which matters more here than headline APY. If you're a California educator choosing between this and a megabank, the fee schedule alone usually settles the argument.

Pros

  • Largest education-focused credit union in the country
  • Products genuinely built around educator pay cycles (summer saver, paycheck planning)
  • Very low fee schedule and free checking
  • Consistently strong member-service ratings

Cons

  • Limited to the California education community and their families
  • Branches concentrated in Southern California
  • Deposit rates are good, not national-leaderboard material

Is your money safe at SchoolsFirst Federal Credit Union?

Yes. SchoolsFirst Federal Credit Union is federally insured by the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA). Your deposits are protected up to $250,000 per share owner, per insured credit union, for each account ownership category — the same dollar protection FDIC insurance gives bank customers.
