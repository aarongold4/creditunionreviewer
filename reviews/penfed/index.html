That open door, combined with online-bank-grade deposit rates, makes PenFed the default national pick for people who don't have a local credit union connection.

Who can join PenFed Credit Union

Open to everyone. PenFed dropped its military-affiliation requirement in 2019 — any US citizen or permanent resident can join by opening a savings account with $5.

Easiest path in: Just open a $5 savings account during the application. No donation, no association fee, no military connection needed. (cost: $5 minimum deposit)

Our take

Functionally, PenFed behaves like a hybrid of an online bank and a traditional credit union. Its money market certificates and Premium Online Savings are priced against Marcus and Ally rather than against the bank branch on the corner, and its credit cards — particularly the Pathfinder and Power Cash Rewards lines — are competitive with major issuers.

The trade-off is service depth. Branches are sparse unless you live near Washington, DC, and member reviews of loan servicing are noticeably more mixed than at smaller credit unions. Treat PenFed as a rate-driven national institution rather than a relationship-driven local one and you'll calibrate your expectations correctly.

Pros

  • Anyone in the US can join — one of the largest truly open credit unions
  • Certificate and online savings rates that compete with internet banks
  • Full product shelf: mortgages, autos, cards, personal loans
  • Heritage of serving military members without requiring military ties

Cons

  • Branch network is thin outside the DC area and a few markets
  • Customer service reviews are mixed, especially for lending
  • Checking rates require direct deposit to hit the advertised tier

Is your money safe at PenFed Credit Union?

Yes. PenFed Credit Union is federally insured by the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA). Your deposits are protected up to $250,000 per share owner, per insured credit union, for each account ownership category — the same dollar protection FDIC insurance gives bank customers.
