First Tech Federal Credit Union Review (2026)

By Nathan Blake · Last verified 2026-11-23

Merger update: First Tech and Digital Federal Credit Union (DCU) legally merged on January 1, 2026 into one credit union, First Technology Federal Credit Union (NCUA charter #23521, per First Tech's merger notice). First Tech and DCU still operate as separate divisions with their own websites, accounts and rates during integration, so this review covers the First Tech division; see our DCU review for the other. Because both are now one insured institution, NCUA coverage of $250,000 per ownership category applies to your First Tech and DCU deposits combined.

Who can join First Tech Federal Credit Union

Employees and family of 1,700+ partner companies (Microsoft, Amazon, HP, Intel and many more), residents of Lane County, Oregon — or anyone in the US via membership in the Computer History Museum or the Financial Fitness Association.

Easiest path in: Anyone can join: add a Computer History Museum or Financial Fitness Association membership within the online application if you lack a tech-employer connection. (cost: $5 minimum balance, plus a Computer History Museum or Financial Fitness Association membership if you lack an employer link (First Tech does not state that membership's cost))

Our take

First Tech built its franchise inside the badge gates of Microsoft, Intel, and HP, and it remains the credit union that best understands technology compensation. If your income arrives as salary plus RSUs, First Tech's mortgage and personal-loan underwriting will read your pay stub correctly — something megabanks still fumble.

What most people miss is that you don't need a tech employer to join: a one-time Computer History Museum or Financial Fitness Association membership, handled during the application, opens the door to anyone in the country. That puts First Tech on the short national list of effectively open credit unions, alongside PenFed, Alliant, and Connexus.

Day to day it behaves like a competent digital-first institution with West Coast and (through DCU) New England branches. Its current Rewards Checking pays only a token rate, so don't choose it for checking yield. The reason to choose First Tech is lending sophistication, not deposit yield.

Pros

  • Open to anyone via a museum or association membership added in the application
  • Mortgage underwriting that actually understands RSUs and tech compensation
  • Rewards Savings tiers for engaged members
  • Branches inside major tech campuses in the Northwest

Cons

  • Rewards Checking now pays a token APY (the old high-rate Dividend Rewards account is closed to new members)
  • Branch network is thin outside the Pacific Northwest tech corridor
  • Some products push you toward e-statements and direct deposit to avoid fees
  • Merged with DCU (Jan 1, 2026): NCUA coverage is shared across both divisions

Is your money safe at First Tech Federal Credit Union?

Yes. First Tech Federal Credit Union is federally insured by the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA). Your deposits are protected up to $250,000 per share owner, per insured credit union, for each account ownership category — the same dollar protection FDIC insurance gives bank customers.
