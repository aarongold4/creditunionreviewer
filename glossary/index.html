Credit Union Glossary: Terms Explained

By Nathan Blake · Last verified 2026-06-07

Credit unions use their own vocabulary — "shares" instead of deposits, "dividends" instead of interest. Here's every term you'll run into, in plain English.

Share account
A credit union's basic savings account. The small balance in it (often $5) represents your ownership 'share' in the cooperative. Functionally it works like a bank savings account and is NCUA insured.
Share draft account
The credit union term for a checking account. 'Share draft' is the technical name; most credit unions just call it checking.
Dividend
What a credit union calls the interest it pays on deposits. Because members are owners, earnings are distributed as dividends rather than 'interest,' though the practical effect is the same.
Field of membership
The group of people a credit union is legally allowed to serve, defined in its charter — based on employer, community (geographic area), or association. You must fall within it to join.
NCUA
The National Credit Union Administration, the federal agency that charters and regulates federal credit unions and runs the insurance fund that protects member deposits up to $250,000.
NCUSIF
The National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund — the NCUA-managed fund, backed by the full faith and credit of the US government, that insures deposits at federally insured credit unions.
Share certificate
A credit union's version of a certificate of deposit (CD). You commit funds for a set term in exchange for a fixed dividend rate; early withdrawal usually forfeits some earnings.
CO-OP / shared branching
Cooperative networks that let credit union members use thousands of other credit unions' branches and ATMs nationwide as if they were their own — offsetting smaller individual branch networks.
Rewards checking
A checking account paying a high APY on balances up to a cap, provided you meet monthly requirements such as a minimum number of debit transactions, direct deposit, and electronic statements.
Charter (federal vs state)
The license under which a credit union operates. Federal charters are regulated by the NCUA; state charters by a state regulator. Both are typically NCUA insured.
Membership share
The small required deposit (commonly $5) that establishes and maintains your ownership stake. It usually must stay in your account while you're a member.
Not-for-profit cooperative
A credit union's structure: owned by members rather than shareholders, returning earnings to members through better rates and lower fees instead of paying outside investors.
APY
Annual Percentage Yield — the real rate of return on a deposit account over a year, including compounding. Use it to compare savings, money market, and certificate offers.
APR
Annual Percentage Rate — the yearly cost of borrowing, including certain fees. Use it to compare loans and credit cards across lenders.
Loan rate cap (18%)
A federal rule capping the interest rate federal credit unions can charge on most loans (currently 18%), a consumer protection banks aren't subject to.
ChexSystems
A reporting system many institutions check when you open a deposit account, tracking prior account closures and overdrafts. A review here doesn't affect your credit score.
