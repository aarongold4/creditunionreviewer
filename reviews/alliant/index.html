Alliant Credit Union Review (2026)

By Nathan Blake · Last verified 2026-06-07

Alliant is the credit union for people who would otherwise pick an online bank. It has no branches, a first-rate app, savings yields that track the national online-bank leaders, and a checking account that rebates ATM fees and refuses to charge overdrafts. The cooperative structure is almost invisible in daily use — in the best way.

Who can join Alliant Credit Union

Employees of partner organizations, residents near Chicago's O'Hare area — or, for everyone else in the US, free membership in Foster Care to Success, with Alliant paying the membership cost on your behalf.

Easiest path in: Anyone can join: select 'Foster Care to Success' during the online application and Alliant covers the fee. It takes about five minutes. (cost: $5 minimum deposit (association fee paid by Alliant))

Our take

Joining is deliberately frictionless: if you lack a geographic or employer connection, you check a box for Foster Care to Success during signup and Alliant pays the membership contribution itself. That makes it, alongside PenFed and Connexus, one of the truly national 'anyone can join' credit unions — and the most digitally polished of the group.

What Alliant is not is a lending powerhouse. Mortgages were discontinued, and the product shelf is intentionally narrow. The play here is simple: use Alliant as your high-yield hub for savings and fee-free checking, and shop loans wherever pricing is best.

Pros

  • Effectively open to anyone in the US — Alliant even pays the qualifying association fee
  • High-yield savings and checking rates that compete with the best online banks
  • No overdraft fees and generous ATM rebates
  • Polished, genuinely good mobile app

Cons

  • Fully digital — no branch network at all
  • Deposit-focused; lending menu is thinner than full-service rivals
  • Phone support hours, while decent, aren't 24/7 for everything

Is your money safe at Alliant Credit Union?

Yes. Alliant Credit Union is federally insured by the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA). Your deposits are protected up to $250,000 per share owner, per insured credit union, for each account ownership category — the same dollar protection FDIC insurance gives bank customers.
