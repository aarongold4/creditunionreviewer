NCUA Insurance: Is Your Money as Safe as in a Bank?

By Nathan Blake · Last verified 2026-06-07

Yes. Federally insured credit unions carry the same $250,000 of federal deposit insurance that FDIC-insured banks do — backed by the full faith and credit of the United States government. The logo is different; the protection is not.

How the $250,000 limit really works

The limit applies per ownership category, which means careful structuring can insure far more than $250,000 at one institution. An individual account, a joint account, and certain retirement accounts are separate categories. A married couple with an individual account each plus a joint account can have $1,000,000 fully insured at a single credit union ($250k + $250k individual, plus $250k each of the joint account).

How to verify a credit union is insured

Look for the official NCUA sign on the website and in branches. Use the NCUA's Credit Union Locator at ncua.gov to confirm any institution's charter and insurance status. Watch for the rare exception: a small number of state-chartered credit unions carry private insurance (typically from ASI) instead of federal NCUA coverage — private insurance is not backed by the government. Every credit union reviewed on this site is federally insured by the NCUA.

What happens if a credit union fails

Failures are rare and usually invisible to members: the NCUA typically arranges a merger with a healthy credit union, and accounts transfer seamlessly. In a liquidation, the NCUA pays insured shares promptly, generally within days. The process mirrors what the FDIC does for banks.

Frequently asked questions

Is NCUA insurance as good as FDIC insurance?

Yes — identical dollar coverage ($250,000 per share owner, per insured credit union, per ownership category) and the same backing: the full faith and credit of the US government. No member has ever lost a penny of NCUA-insured funds.

How do I check if my credit union is NCUA insured?

Use the NCUA's Credit Union Locator at ncua.gov, or look for the official NCUA insurance sign on the credit union's website and branches. Every credit union reviewed on this site is federally insured.

Are all credit unions federally insured?

Nearly all, but not every single one. A small number of state-chartered credit unions carry private insurance instead of NCUA coverage. Private insurance is not backed by the federal government, so it's worth verifying before opening an account.

Can I insure more than $250,000 at one credit union?

Yes, through ownership categories. Individual accounts, joint accounts, and certain retirement accounts are insured separately, so a couple using individual plus joint accounts can have $1 million or more fully insured at a single institution.
