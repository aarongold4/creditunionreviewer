1. Alliant Credit Union — best for digital-first savers anywhere in the us

4.7/5 · Excellent

Chicago, IL · 900,000+ members · State charter · NCUA insured

Alliant is the credit union for people who would otherwise pick an online bank. It has no branches, a first-rate app, savings yields that track the national online-bank leaders, and a checking account that rebates ATM fees and refuses to charge overdrafts. The cooperative structure is almost invisible in daily use — in the best way.

Pros

  • Effectively open to anyone in the US — Alliant even pays the qualifying association fee
  • High-yield savings and checking rates that compete with the best online banks
  • No overdraft fees and generous ATM rebates
  • Polished, genuinely good mobile app

Cons

  • Fully digital — no branch network at all
  • Deposit-focused; lending menu is thinner than full-service rivals
  • Phone support hours, while decent, aren't 24/7 for everything

Who can join: Employees of partner organizations, residents near Chicago's O'Hare area — or, for everyone else in the US, free membership in Foster Care to Success, with Alliant paying the membership cost on your behalf.

2. PenFed Credit Union — best for anyone, anywhere in the us, who wants top-tier rates

4.6/5 · Excellent

McLean, VA · 2.9 million+ members · Federal charter · NCUA insured

PenFed is the easiest answer to the most common credit union question: 'can I even join one?' Since 2019 the answer here is yes, full stop — a $5 savings deposit makes anyone a member. That open door, combined with online-bank-grade deposit rates, makes PenFed the default national pick for people who don't have a local credit union connection.

Pros

  • Anyone in the US can join — one of the largest truly open credit unions
  • Certificate and online savings rates that compete with internet banks
  • Full product shelf: mortgages, autos, cards, personal loans
  • Heritage of serving military members without requiring military ties

Cons

  • Branch network is thin outside the DC area and a few markets
  • Customer service reviews are mixed, especially for lending
  • Checking rates require direct deposit to hit the advertised tier

Who can join: Open to everyone. PenFed dropped its military-affiliation requirement in 2019 — any US citizen or permanent resident can join by opening a savings account with $5.

9. Navy Federal Credit Union — best for military members, veterans, and their extended families

4.2/5 · Very good

Vienna, VA · 14 million+ members · Federal charter · NCUA insured

Navy Federal is the credit union most Americans have actually heard of, and for good reason: it is roughly three times the size of its nearest competitor and behaves like a full-service national bank that happens to return its profits to members. For military families it is close to a default choice — early pay deposit, base-adjacent branches, and lending products built around deployment realities like the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act.

Pros

  • Largest credit union in the country with 350+ branches, many on or near bases worldwide
  • Certificate specials and auto rates regularly among the best available anywhere
  • Very generous family eligibility — one veteran in the family tree can unlock membership for many relatives
  • 24/7 phone support staffed for military time zones

Cons

  • No path to membership without a military or DoD connection
  • Savings account base rate is unremarkable — the good yields are in certificates
  • So large that service can feel like a big bank at times

Who can join: Active duty, retired, and veteran members of all branches of the armed forces, Department of Defense civilian employees and contractors, and immediate family and household members of any of the above. Family eligibility extends broadly — parents, grandparents, siblings, children, and grandchildren of someone eligible can all join.

Frequently asked questions

What is the #1 credit union in America?

By size, Navy Federal Credit Union is by far the largest US credit union, with over 13 million members. 'Best' depends on what you need: PenFed and Alliant lead for nationwide accessibility, while regional standouts like SECU North Carolina or BECU may be stronger if you qualify.

Are credit unions safer than banks?

They're equally safe. Federally insured credit unions are covered by the NCUA up to $250,000 per share owner, per institution, per ownership category — the same protection FDIC insurance gives bank deposits, backed by the US government.

Can I join a credit union if I don't qualify through work or location?

Usually yes. Many credit unions — including PenFed, Alliant, and over a dozen others we review — let anyone join through a one-time charity donation of $5–$25 or a free association membership.

Do credit unions really have better rates than banks?

On average, yes. Credit unions consistently beat bank averages on savings, CDs, auto loans, and credit card rates because they return profits to members. The best online banks are competitive on savings rates, though, so it pays to compare both.
