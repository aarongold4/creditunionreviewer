How We Review Credit Unions

By Nathan Blake · Last verified 2026-06-07

Every credit union on this site is evaluated against the same checklist, by the same reviewer, with no input from the institutions themselves. Here's exactly what we look at.

The five factors

1. Deposit rates. We compare savings, money market, and certificate rates against national credit union and bank averages. We care most about rates on the accounts ordinary members actually use — not promotional teasers with fine-print requirements.

2. Loan pricing. Auto loans, mortgages, credit cards, and personal loans, compared against market averages. Federal credit unions' 18% rate ceiling on most loans is a real consumer protection, and we note where institutions price well below it.

3. Fees. Monthly maintenance fees, overdraft charges, ATM policies, and the sneaky stuff — inactivity fees, paper statement charges. Free checking with no minimums earns points; fee-riddled accounts lose them.

4. Membership accessibility. How easy is it to actually join? Statewide community charters and anyone-can-join paths score high. We verify every eligibility claim against the credit union's own membership page and record the date.

5. Digital experience. App store ratings, online account opening, remote deposit, and digital wallet support. Credit unions are co-ops, not tech companies — but in 2026 a usable app is table stakes.

The five factors

Every credit union we review in full gets a score from 1 to 5 on each of five factors. The overall rating is a weighted average, shown as stars and a number on the review and in our comparison tables:

Rates (30%). Deposit APYs (savings, money market, certificates) and loan APRs versus national averages. The single heaviest factor, because rates are where the member-owned model shows up in your pocket.

Fees (20%). Monthly maintenance, overdraft, ATM, and the sneaky stuff. Free checking with no minimums scores high.

Membership access (20%). How easy it is to actually join. Anyone-can-join and statewide community charters score high; narrow employer-only fields score lower — a great credit union you can't join isn't much use to you.

Digital experience (15%). App ratings, online account opening, remote deposit, and digital wallet support.

Products (15%). Breadth and quality of the lineup — checking, savings, lending, business services.

We publish the per-factor breakdown on every review so you can see exactly where a score comes from and weight it for your own priorities — someone who only wants a CD can ignore the digital score, for instance.

Scores are editorial assessments, not the credit unions' own claims, and no institution can pay to raise one. They're anchored to the rate and fee data we track and re-checked when that data changes.

What we don't do

We don't accept payment for placement in our rankings. We don't let affiliate relationships (disclosed on every page where they exist) influence scores. We don't copy press releases. And we don't pretend to be comprehensive: with over 4,000 credit unions in America, we focus on the 100 most significant and most joinable.

Verification and updates

Each data page carries a "last verified" date. Rates change weekly at some institutions, so we treat our rate descriptions as directional and always tell you to confirm current numbers before opening an account. Membership requirements, donation amounts, and fee schedules are re-checked on a rolling basis.
