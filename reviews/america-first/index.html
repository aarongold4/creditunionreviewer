Who can join America First Credit Union

Anyone who lives, works, worships, attends school, or volunteers in qualifying areas of Utah, Nevada, Arizona, Idaho, Oregon, and New Mexico, plus relatives of current members.

Easiest path in: The geographic footprint is huge across the Mountain West — most residents of Utah and Nevada qualify outright. (cost: $1 minimum deposit — one of the cheapest joins anywhere)

Our take

Its most underrated products are its flexible certificates. Add-on certificates let you keep contributing after opening — rare in the industry and ideal for savers building toward a goal at a locked rate — while bump options hedge against rising rates. For disciplined savers this flexibility can beat a slightly higher headline APY elsewhere.

Auto lending is the other pillar, with pricing that consistently undercuts regional banks. The main limitation is simply geography: live outside the six-state zone with no qualifying family member, and America First is off the table.

Pros

  • $1 membership deposit and broad six-state eligibility
  • Add-on and bump-rate certificates are unusually flexible
  • Strong auto loan rates across the Mountain West
  • Large branch and ATM network in Utah and Nevada

Cons

  • No path to join from outside the six-state footprint
  • Base savings rate is low — yields live in certificates
  • Branch density drops quickly outside Utah/Nevada

Is your money safe at America First Credit Union?

Yes. America First Credit Union is federally insured by the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA). Your deposits are protected up to $250,000 per share owner, per insured credit union, for each account ownership category — the same dollar protection FDIC insurance gives bank customers.
