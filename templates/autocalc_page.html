How the calculation works

The payment is a standard amortizing loan formula: your principal, the monthly interest rate (APR ÷ 12), and the number of months determine a fixed monthly payment. Early payments go mostly to interest; later ones mostly to principal. Total interest is what the loan costs you beyond the amount borrowed.

Why the APR matters more than the term

Stretching to a longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest — sometimes dramatically. The bigger lever is the rate itself. Because credit unions often price auto loans well below banks (and federal credit unions cap most loan rates at 18%), shaving even a point or two off your APR can save more than adding two years to the term ever would, without the extra interest. Try it: drop the APR by 1.5% and watch total interest fall while the payment barely moves.
