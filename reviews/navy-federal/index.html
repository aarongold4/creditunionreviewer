Navy Federal Credit Union Review (2026)

By Nathan Blake · Last verified 2026-06-07

Navy Federal is the credit union most Americans have actually heard of, and for good reason: it is roughly three times the size of its nearest competitor and behaves like a full-service national bank that happens to return its profits to members. For military families it is close to a default choice — early pay deposit, base-adjacent branches, and lending products built around deployment realities like the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act.

Who can join Navy Federal Credit Union

Active duty, retired, and veteran members of all branches of the armed forces, Department of Defense civilian employees and contractors, and immediate family and household members of any of the above. Family eligibility extends broadly — parents, grandparents, siblings, children, and grandchildren of someone eligible can all join.

Easiest path in: If anyone in your family ever served in the military, you almost certainly qualify through them — even if they never joined Navy Federal themselves. (cost: $5 minimum deposit to a savings account)

Our take

The catch is the front door. Navy Federal has no donation workaround, no association membership trick, and no open charter. You either have a military or DoD connection — including through immediate family — or you don't get in. Before assuming you're excluded, though, walk your family tree: a parent or grandparent who served is enough, and household members of eligible people also qualify.

Where Navy Federal earns its reputation is pricing on the products that matter. Its certificate specials routinely top national leaderboards, its auto loans undercut most banks, and its credit cards carry APR ranges capped below the industry norm of 29%-plus. If you qualify, it belongs on your shortlist even if you keep another credit union for daily banking.

Pros

  • Largest credit union in the country with 350+ branches, many on or near bases worldwide
  • Certificate specials and auto rates regularly among the best available anywhere
  • Very generous family eligibility — one veteran in the family tree can unlock membership for many relatives
  • 24/7 phone support staffed for military time zones

Cons

  • No path to membership without a military or DoD connection
  • Savings account base rate is unremarkable — the good yields are in certificates
  • So large that service can feel like a big bank at times

Is your money safe at Navy Federal Credit Union?

Yes. Navy Federal Credit Union is federally insured by the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA). Your deposits are protected up to $250,000 per share owner, per insured credit union, for each account ownership category — the same dollar protection FDIC insurance gives bank customers.
