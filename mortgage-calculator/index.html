What this includes (and what it doesn't)

The result is principal and interest only — the part of your payment that pays down the loan. Your real monthly housing cost also includes property taxes, homeowners insurance, private mortgage insurance (PMI) if your down payment is under 20%, and any HOA dues. Those can add hundreds of dollars a month, so treat this figure as the loan portion, not your full payment.

Why the rate is worth shopping

Over a 30-year loan, a small rate difference compounds into tens of thousands of dollars. Credit unions often carry lower fees and competitive rates, and because they're member-owned, the savings flow back to you. Frequently asked questions

Does this include taxes and insurance?

No — it shows principal and interest only. Your full monthly payment also includes property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI (if you put down less than 20%), and any HOA dues.

How much does the interest rate matter?

A lot. Over 30 years, even a half-point difference in rate can change total interest by tens of thousands of dollars. It's worth comparing a credit union's rate against other lenders.

Should I choose a 15- or 30-year term?

A 15-year loan has higher monthly payments but far less total interest and builds equity faster. A 30-year loan lowers the monthly payment at the cost of more interest. Try both in the calculator.

Is the pre-filled rate a real quote?

No — it's the lowest representative 30-year rate among the credit unions we review, as a starting point. Get an actual Loan Estimate for your real rate.
