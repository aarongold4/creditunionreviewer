Standard vs. Promotional Certificates: Why Your Credit Union's 'Regular' CD Can Pay So Little

By Nathan Blake · Last verified 2026-11-23

Many credit unions run two certificate menus side by side: a standard rate sheet that rarely changes and a short list of specials that pay far more. If you open a certificate without asking for the special, you can lock in a rate well below what the same credit union advertises on its home page.

What we found on the rate sheets

We read the certificate rates on each fully reviewed credit union's own website (rates as of 2026-11-23). Among the 26 we track, the standard 12-month certificate pays under 2.50% APY at: Idaho Central Credit Union (0.70%), Consumers Credit Union (Illinois) (0.90%), FourLeaf Federal Credit Union (formerly Bethpage) (1.25%), San Diego County Credit Union (SDCCU) (1.50%), Security Service Federal Credit Union (2.15%), BECU (Boeing Employees' Credit Union) (2.25%). Several of those same credit unions advertise specials well above that.

Why credit unions price certificates this way

A special lets a credit union raise deposits for a particular term, often an odd one such as 7, 9, 15 or 19 months, without repricing every certificate it already holds. The standard sheet covers the terms it is not actively trying to fund, and those rates can sit well below the market. It is a pricing choice, not a sign the credit union is in trouble.

The conditions to check before you open a special

  • Odd terms. Specials often use non-standard terms. Make sure the maturity date suits you, because early withdrawal penalties usually apply.
  • Relationship requirements. Some specials need a checking account, direct deposit or a balance elsewhere at the credit union.
  • Minimums and maximums. A special may need a higher opening deposit or cap how much you can put in.
  • New money only. Some specials apply only to funds not already on deposit at the credit union.
  • What happens at maturity. Many certificates renew automatically into a standard term at the standard rate. Set a reminder to move the money or pick a new term when it matures.

How to avoid the low standard rate

Look for a "specials" or "featured" section on the credit union's certificate page before choosing a term, and ask for the promotional rate if you apply at a branch. The bottom line

At many credit unions the advertised certificate rate and the standard one are very different numbers. Check the specials, read the conditions, and plan for maturity so your money does not roll into a low standard rate.
