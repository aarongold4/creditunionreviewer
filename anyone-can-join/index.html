How "anyone can join" actually works

Federal law requires every credit union to define a field of membership — but it also lets credit unions serve members of partner associations and charities. So many credit unions sponsor a foundation, and joining that foundation (often with a one-time $5–$25 donation that goes to a real cause) makes you eligible. It's completely legitimate: the credit union typically handles the donation during account opening, and you never have to interact with the association again.

Two things to verify before you open an account: the current donation amount (these change), and whether the credit union requires you to keep a small share balance (usually $5) to maintain membership. Details for each are in our reviews, and we list the last-verified date on every page.

Frequently asked questions

Is joining a credit union through a charity donation legitimate?

Completely. Federal credit union rules allow membership through affiliated associations, and the donations fund real nonprofits. The credit union usually processes the donation during account opening — it's a standard, regulator-approved practice.

What's the cheapest credit union anyone can join?

Blue Federal Credit Union's path is among the cheapest: a one-time $5 donation to the Blue Foundation. Several others, including PenFed, currently require no donation at all — just open a share account.

Do I have to keep donating to stay a member?

No. The donation is one-time. Once you're a member, you stay a member as long as you keep your share account open (usually a $5 minimum balance), even if you move or change jobs.

Will joining multiple credit unions hurt my credit?

Opening deposit accounts generally doesn't affect your credit score. Some credit unions run a soft credit check or ChexSystems review at account opening, which doesn't impact your score. Applying for loans or credit cards is a different story — those involve hard inquiries.
